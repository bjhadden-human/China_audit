Equal-likelihood tail construction: retain the fitted hazard through the maximum observed age L, then set h(a)=h(L)*exp[-k(a-L)] for a>L. No observation exits beyond L, so changing k beyond that point cannot alter any observed event density or exposure integral. Choose k to achieve target remaining hazard. Broad L=102 admits 40% or 80% eventual-loss constructions; narrow L=72 admits 25% or 75%. In narrow strata the conditioning age 77 is already beyond observed support.

The original script wrote the likelihood-difference column as a constant zero. Here observed likelihood is recomputed; differences are numerical roundoff, with maximum magnitude below 10^-10. A persistent strictly positive hazard floor makes the infinite integral diverge, hence eventual loss=1 by assumption. An exponentially decaying hazard has a finite integral and a point mass on indefinite retention. No finite historical sample can nonparametrically distinguish these infinite futures. More citations or bootstrap replications cannot close that identification problem. It is resolved here by showing exactly which assumption determines the conclusion, not by inventing evidence about an unobserved infinite future.
